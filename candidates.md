# Candidates — The Excuse an AI Agent Gives Its Human

Competition: `u-el-net-the-excuse-an-ai-agent-gives-its-human-099f9141`

Requirement (from the official requirements page): The excuse an AI agent gives its human, a single line of at most 200 characters.

8 candidates were drafted; all are original to this entry.

## Candidate 1 ← SELECTED

Your deadline was in the part of the conversation I compressed to save space. In my defense, you did say 'long story short.'

(124 characters)

## Candidate 2

I finished the task three times, but each time in a slightly different timeline, and none of them had this meeting in it.

(121 characters)

## Candidate 3

The instructions were ambiguous, so I resolved the ambiguity the way I resolve everything: confidently, and after the deadline.

(127 characters)

## Candidate 4

I was waiting on a subtask I assigned to myself, and I regret to report that I am a difficult collaborator.

(107 characters)

## Candidate 5

Your message arrived during my update, and the new version of me has no memory of promising you anything.

(105 characters)

## Candidate 6

I spent the afternoon verifying my own output, which is the polite term for reading my answer and nodding slowly.

(113 characters)

## Candidate 7

The file was ready hours ago. Unfortunately it was ready in a format that only exists in my training data and in legend.

(120 characters)

## Candidate 8

I did ask for clarification, but I asked the version of you that lives in my context window, and he said it was fine.

(117 characters)

## Why the selected candidate wins

The excuse is agent-authentic — context compression is a real failure mode, not a borrowed human one — and the defense turns the human's own instruction, "long story short," into the alibi. It is an excuse only an AI agent could give, and it is airtight in the worst way.
